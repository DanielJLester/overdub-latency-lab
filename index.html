Overdub Latency Lab

A throwaway test for the StickyNotes overdub idea. Can this device record a new part while playing an existing one back, in time, and without the playback leaking into the recording? Nothing here is uploaded: results stay on this device until you copy or download them.

1 · Setup

Pick the device's built-in mic where possible. Recording from a Bluetooth headset's mic switches it to call mode.
The mic in use looks like a Bluetooth headset mic. That switches the headphones to call mode (low-quality, mono) and changes the delay. Pick the built-in mic above if it's listed. On iPhone you often can't, so note it in your results.

2 · Test A: automatic delay meter

Plays 8 short chirps and listens for them, to measure the delay from playing a sound to recording it. Run it 2–3 times per setup.

  1. Speaker: phone flat on a table, volume about 70%.
  2. Headphones (wired or Bluetooth), delay: hold one earbud against the device's mic (usually the bottom edge), volume about 70%.
  3. Headphones, leakage: wear them normally. "Not heard" is the result you want.

3 · Test B: record over a track

Plays a 24-second drum loop (80 BPM) while recording you. Clap or tap on every beat (or play an instrument). Then play both back together and judge whether it sounds in time.
